## Progressive Overload

Progressive overload is the foundational principle of strength training. It means consistently
increasing the demand placed on your muscles over time. This can be achieved by:
- Increasing weight lifted
- Adding more reps or sets
- Reducing rest periods
- Improving exercise technique and range of motion

Without progressive overload, muscles adapt and stop growing. Track your workouts in a log
to ensure consistent progression week over week.

## The Big Compound Lifts

Compound lifts recruit multiple muscle groups simultaneously and produce the greatest hormonal
response and overall strength adaptation.

### Squat
The squat is the king of lower body exercises. Primary muscles: quadriceps, glutes, hamstrings.
Secondary: lower back, core. Key cues: keep chest tall, knees tracking over toes, hips
below parallel for full range of motion. Begin with bodyweight, then goblet squat, then barbell.

### Deadlift
The deadlift builds total posterior chain strength: hamstrings, glutes, lower back, traps.
Setup: bar over mid-foot, hip-width stance, double overhand grip. Hinge at hips, keep spine
neutral, drive floor away. Do not round the lower back under heavy loads.

### Bench Press
Primary: pectorals, anterior deltoids, triceps. Cues: retract scapulae, feet flat on floor,
bar path slightly diagonal, touch lower chest lightly, full lockout at top.

### Overhead Press
Builds shoulder strength and stability. Primary: deltoids, triceps. Secondary: upper traps,
core. Press bar in a straight vertical path, lock out elbows at top, brace core throughout.

### Barbell Row
Essential for back development and postural balance. Primary: latissimus dorsi, rhomboids,
rear deltoids. Keep torso at 45 degrees, pull bar to lower chest, squeeze shoulder blades.

## Training Frequency and Volume

Beginners (0–1 year): Full body 3x/week. 3 sets per exercise, 8–12 reps. Focus on form.
Intermediate (1–3 years): Upper/lower split 4x/week. 4 sets per exercise, 6–12 reps.
Advanced (3+ years): Push/pull/legs or bro-split 5–6x/week. Higher volume needed for stimulus.

Minimum Effective Volume (MEV): The least amount of training that produces progress.
Maximum Adaptive Volume (MAV): The sweet spot for growth.
Maximum Recoverable Volume (MRV): Beyond this, recovery is compromised.

Most lifters operate between 10–20 sets per muscle group per week.

## Rest and Recovery

Muscle is built during recovery, not during the workout itself. Workout causes micro-tears;
sleep and nutrition repair and reinforce those fibers.

Sleep: Aim for 7–9 hours. Growth hormone is predominantly released during deep sleep.
Active recovery: Light walking, swimming, or yoga on rest days improves blood flow without taxing
the central nervous system.

Deload weeks: Every 4–8 weeks, reduce volume by 40–50% to allow full systemic recovery.

## Beginner 3-Day Full Body Program (Weeks 1–8)

Day A (Monday):
- Squat 3x5
- Bench Press 3x5
- Barbell Row 3x5

Day B (Wednesday):
- Squat 3x5
- Overhead Press 3x5
- Deadlift 1x5

Day C (Friday):
- Squat 3x5
- Bench Press 3x5
- Barbell Row 3x5

Increase weight by 2.5 kg per session for upper body, 5 kg for lower body.
When you fail to complete reps for 3 consecutive sessions, deload 10% and rebuild.

## Warm-Up Protocol

Never skip warm-up. A proper warm-up:
1. 5 minutes of light cardio (rowing, cycling)
2. Dynamic stretching: leg swings, arm circles, hip circles
3. Activation: glute bridges, band pull-aparts, face pulls
4. Ramp sets: 50% × 8, 70% × 5, 85% × 3, then working weight

## Common Mistakes

1. Ego lifting: Using weight too heavy for proper form, increasing injury risk
2. Skipping leg day: Creates muscular imbalance and hormonal disadvantage
3. Not tracking: Without data, there is no progressive overload
4. Insufficient protein: Muscles cannot grow without adequate amino acid supply
5. Too much cardio during a bulk: Interferes with recovery and muscle protein synthesis

## Cardiovascular Fitness

Cardio and strength training are complementary, not conflicting.
Zone 2 cardio (60–70% max HR) improves mitochondrial density and fat oxidation.
HIIT (high-intensity intervals) improves VO2 max and metabolic conditioning.

Recommended: 2–3 sessions of 30–45 minutes Zone 2 cardio per week alongside strength training.
Keep cardio on separate days or after strength training to avoid compromising power output.

## Body Composition Goals

Cutting (fat loss): Caloric deficit of 300–500 kcal/day. Maintain strength training intensity
to preserve muscle. Protein intake critical — aim for 2.2g/kg.

Bulking (muscle gain): Caloric surplus of 200–300 kcal/day. Expect 0.25–0.5 kg weight gain
per week. Any faster and excess will be stored as fat.

Maintenance/body recomposition: Possible for beginners and returning lifters to simultaneously
lose fat and gain muscle in a small caloric window around maintenance.
