## Macronutrients Overview

Macronutrients are the three primary fuel sources your body uses: protein, carbohydrates, and fats.
Each plays a distinct role in supporting exercise, recovery, and overall health.

### Protein
Protein is essential for muscle repair and growth. Active individuals should target 1.6 to 2.2 grams
of protein per kilogram of body weight daily. High-quality sources include chicken breast, eggs,
Greek yogurt, lentils, tofu, and cottage cheese.

Timing matters: consuming 20–40 grams of protein within 2 hours post-workout maximizes muscle
protein synthesis. This is sometimes called the anabolic window.

### Carbohydrates
Carbohydrates are the body's preferred energy source during high-intensity exercise. Complex carbs
like oats, brown rice, sweet potatoes, and quinoa provide sustained energy. Simple carbs like
bananas or white rice are better suited for immediate pre- or post-workout fuel.

Aim for 3–5 grams of carbohydrates per kilogram of bodyweight on training days, and 2–3 grams
on rest days. Low-carb diets can impair performance in strength and endurance athletes.

### Fats
Healthy fats support hormone production, joint health, and absorption of fat-soluble vitamins
(A, D, E, K). Prioritize unsaturated fats: avocado, olive oil, nuts, seeds, and fatty fish
like salmon. Limit saturated fats to less than 10% of total daily calories.

## Caloric Needs

Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) is calculated by multiplying your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)
by an activity multiplier:
- Sedentary (little or no exercise): BMR × 1.2
- Lightly active (1–3 days/week): BMR × 1.375
- Moderately active (3–5 days/week): BMR × 1.55
- Very active (6–7 days/week): BMR × 1.725
- Extra active (physical job + hard training): BMR × 1.9

For weight loss, create a deficit of 300–500 calories below TDEE. For muscle gain, add 200–300
calories above TDEE. Aggressive cuts or bulks increase risk of muscle loss or fat gain.

## Hydration

Dehydration of just 2% body weight can impair both cognitive and physical performance.
Aim for at least 35 ml of water per kilogram of body weight daily. Add 500–750 ml for
each hour of moderate-to-intense exercise.

Electrolytes — sodium, potassium, and magnesium — are lost in sweat. During sessions
exceeding 60 minutes, electrolyte replenishment is important.

## Meal Timing

Pre-workout meal (2–3 hours before): Balanced meal with complex carbs, moderate protein,
and low fat and fiber to avoid GI distress.

Pre-workout snack (30–60 minutes before): Simple carbs + small amount of protein.
Example: banana + protein shake.

Post-workout (within 2 hours): Protein + carbohydrates to replenish glycogen and
initiate muscle repair. Example: rice + grilled chicken + vegetables.

## Micronutrients for Athletes

Iron: Critical for oxygen transport. Deficiency leads to fatigue. Found in red meat, spinach,
lentils. Pair with vitamin C for better absorption.

Vitamin D: Supports bone health and immune function. Many athletes are deficient, especially
those training indoors. 1000–4000 IU daily supplementation is commonly recommended.

Magnesium: Involved in over 300 enzymatic reactions. Supports sleep quality and muscle
relaxation. Found in nuts, seeds, leafy greens, and dark chocolate.

Zinc: Supports testosterone levels and immune function. Found in oysters, beef, pumpkin seeds.

## Sample Meal Plan (2500 kcal, 180g protein)

Breakfast: 4 eggs scrambled + 1 cup oats + 1 cup berries + black coffee
Snack: Greek yogurt (200g) + 1 tablespoon honey + 10 almonds
Lunch: 200g grilled chicken + 1 cup brown rice + roasted broccoli + olive oil
Snack: Protein shake (30g protein) + 1 banana
Dinner: 200g salmon + sweet potato + steamed spinach + avocado

Estimated macros: 180g protein / 250g carbs / 80g fat / ~2500 kcal
